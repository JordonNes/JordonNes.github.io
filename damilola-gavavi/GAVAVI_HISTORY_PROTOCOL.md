# Gavavi historical comparison r1

This is a live, experimental descriptive heuristic, not a validated forecast, investment recommendation, or execution signal. No orders are placed. Prior Damilola calibration and BTC feature experiments did not establish a holdout edge; the BTC dip/rebound exit test had negative conditional results after modeled costs. This heuristic does not override those findings or claim expected profit.

Each of the 21 supported Kalshi 15-minute UP/DOWN series gets a paired YES quote midpoint graph. The x-axis covers one specific 15-minute contract and the y-axis is 0–100 cents. This is a contract probability-price graph, not an underlying price graph. Missing minute quotes are gaps, never interpolated. If current candles are unavailable, a previous archived contract may be shown with its ticker and a conspicuous previous-contract label. Existing Kraken spot charts remain under Context & evidence for the four supported assets.

The read-only `/api/card-history?series=...` endpoint loads the current official contract and completed one-minute bid/ask candles. It caches responses for 60 seconds and uses the existing shared/coalesced Kalshi coordinator and cooldown. It reads existing R2 observations without initiating a historical exchange backfill on page views: five completed UTC clock hours plus archived hours matching the current Pacific hour within 30 days, at most 36 hour snapshots. R2 listing is bounded to three pages of 1,000 keys. Actual coverage and listing truncation are returned. A 30-day search does not imply that 30 days have been collected. Current-hour completed contracts may be absent from the completed-hour archive. Five-hour coverage is therefore explicitly incomplete until observed, and there is no fabricated month of data.

Fixed comparison, with no fitting or outcome-dependent cohort selection:

- Decision at the latest observed completed minute, only minutes 3–12, with three consecutive valid paired quote minutes. Current observations older than 120 seconds, mismatched tickers, unverified YES-to-UP mapping, expired contracts or stale response/quote snapshots cannot highlight a card.
- Match historical contracts of the same series and exact 900-second window, whose independent settlement timestamp is after their close and no later than the current contract's open. Dedupe tickers. Use the same elapsed minute, midpoint within 10 cents, and the same three-minute direction: rising by at least 2 cents, falling by at least 2 cents, otherwise flat.
- Prefer the trailing five-hour cohort when it has at least eight matches. Otherwise use the same-Pacific-hour cohort within 30 days. Do not choose the cohort with the better outcome. Report same-weekday-and-hour matches separately; they do not change the decision.
- An experimental UP or DOWN suggestion requires at least eight matches, a majority fraction of at least 70%, and a descriptive 95% Wilson lower bound greater than 50%. Otherwise show WAIT. These are descriptive screening thresholds, not a predictive confidence interval, calibrated probability, proof of independence or out-of-sample validation. Sparse, nonrandom archive coverage and testing many markets/minutes can bias the result. UI shows UP/DOWN counts and cohort, not a forecast probability.

Yellow plus text marks only the qualifying experimental ending direction. The contemplated dollar field accepts 1–999, stays between UP and DOWN, and survives quote redraws/filter changes in memory. It does not select a trade, size an order, alter the direction model or claim a net return. Cash-out time and auto-sell target remain null/— because no supported net exit edge or reliable fill/liquidity model has been established. The current exit-study evidence remains linked.

This heuristic has not yet accumulated an immutable prospective suggestion ledger or an independent holdout comparison. Its yellow display must remain explicitly experimental; promotion to a validated model requires a separate frozen evaluation and release. A notification check can report readiness but does not train or develop a model in the background.
